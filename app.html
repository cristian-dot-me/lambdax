Why NISAR matters for the entire planet

The importance of the NISAR project and SAR technology in our lives is greater than we think. Thanks to physical and electrical phenomena discovered hundreds of years ago and mathematical equations that many of us can understand without university degrees, we can see through clouds and dense foliage without losing quality. Using the "eyes" provided by the electric field, we have the ability to perform risk analyses of vast areas in just minutes with a group of experts. The purpose of this project is to show how a technology that may seem 'complex' to an everyday citizen is actually a set of physical applications that are easy to understand on their own, and together help us better understand our planet and help one another. -> LambdaX

Note on image accuracy

Images officially processed by USA SPACE AGENCY (such as those shown in this tab) are much more accurate than those shown in our Before/After comparisons. We use preview images from NISAR and recolor them with a simplified moisture model (dry red to wet blue), designed so anyone can understand it. In contrast, teams work with the full satellite data, calibrated and terrain-corrected.

What each color means (from dry Red to wet Blue)

Red indicates dry soil; as moisture increases, the color progressively shifts to increasingly blue tones:

Deep Navy Blue — Open water (100%)
Rivers, lagoons, reservoirs, or standing water
Intense Blue — Flooded area (81–99%)
Riverbanks or land covered by water
Bright Blue — Flooded vegetation (72–92%)
Trees or vegetation with water underneath
Medium Blue — Very wet soil (48–76%)
Saturated soil holding high water content
Light Sky Blue — Normal moisture (26–48%)
Moist earth favorable for crops and woodland
Light Red — Low moisture (12–26%)
Soil with low surface moisture
Intense Red — Dry soil (<12%)
Arid land, roads, or urban area
